A construction, not a migration. Core's configure() accepts buttons and removeOnClose in the same object, so opening is one call: ModalSaveCancel.create({title, body, removeOnClose: true, buttons: {save: addlabel, cancel: closelabel}}) (related_competencies.js:239-244). No new string for the button: central_browseframeworks_add ("Add selected") is the same string as before (:225), and "Close" is core's closebuttontitle (:226) — relabelled from "Cancel" because this modal manages in place and has nothing to cancel; the comment in the source says so (:236-238).
Disabled-until-checked is one line. updateAddButton (related_competencies.js:125-127) is state.modal.setButtonDisabled('save', getCheckedIds(state).length === 0) — no state.addbtnEl, no querySelector in the body. The button is born disabled (:246), recomputes on every click and change of the list (:284-285, registered after the browser's handler so the row's toggle is already applied when the state is recalculated) and goes back to disabled during the addition itself (:192). setButtonDisabled(action, disabled) is public in core — a shorter route than getFooter() + getActionSelector('save') — and competency_browser.js:48-50 uses the same shape.
The caveat the "almost free" hides — and it is not optional. ModalSaveCancel registers registerCloseOnSave(), and core's handler closes the dialog after ModalEvents.save — unless someone calls a synchronous preventDefault() (core fires the event and only closes if !saveEvent.isDefaultPrevented()). This modal must not close on "Add selected": the toast, the new row's flash (js:209) and the empty state happen in place, and the user goes back to the tree to add more. So this ModalEvents.save (js:290-296) calls preventDefault() unconditionally as its first statement (:294), and the comment in the source records why (:291-293).
The contrast with the neighbouring modal, which is this screen's doctrine: conditional vs. unconditional. competency_browser.js — the only place in the plugin that calls setSaveButtonText (:107) — passes the event through the wiring (:144) and addSelected prevents (:66), but conditionally: only inside the if (!calls.length) (:64-68). It blocks an empty selection and, on a real addition, closes. That is right for it: it is a one-shot picker that hands the selection back to a pane. The related-competencies modal is the third case — it manages, writes on every click and stays —, so its preventDefault prevents always, including on the addition that succeeded. The setSaveButtonText call is reused between the two; the save wiring is not.
What the footer does not solve. The 40vh cap on the tree box (styles.css:7670-7673) exists so the relation rows below it stay reachable — not because of the button —, and the comment in the source says the "Browse structures" modal leaves the same box without a cap (:7278-7283). And the infinite-scroll sentinel lives inside the box on purpose (browser.js:487-491): a sentinel outside it would always intersect and load every page at once. Pagination stays tied to that box's scrolling.
Removal is the only action with a confirmation, and it hands focus back. removeRelated (js:154-179) asks for a Notification.deleteCancelPromise (:162), removes the row, re-renders the tree so the competency becomes pickable again (:174) and then calls restoreFocus (:177) — the confirm dialog had handed focus back to the button of a row that no longer exists. restoreFocus (:137-145) falls into the filter field ([data-region="filter"]) when there is no better target. The addition does the same for another reason (:207): disabling the focused button drops focus on the <body>.
Measurement note — in the rendered DOM, not on paper. This file's token decisions, with the number that supports each of them on both surfaces (DS light and DS dark). Three of the defects below appeared only by measuring, and they were inherited from the kit's siblings:
• Invisible unchecked box, and the switch's track — in the DS palette --border-strong and --border-stronger are the same literal (#ced4da / #6a737b) and give 1.49:1 and 3.36:1 against the modal surface: light fails the 3:1 of 1.4.11. --text-secondary passes on both, with 4.83:1 and 10.84:1.
• Primary fill in dark — the fixed pair #185fa5/white passes on the LABEL (6.52:1) and that is why a text check approves it; the fill came up against the dark surface at 2.48:1. Fixed with the blue-200 inversion: #0c2740 on #85b7eb gives 7.23:1 of label and 7.69:1 of fill. The fill's ruler is not the text's — that is the trap that almost shipped.
• Danger — the 🗑 does not land on any tint, so the DS's --text-danger serves directly: 5.29:1 and 5.91:1.
What was NOT touched is on the record: the tints stay low on purpose — what identifies these boxes is the border and the glyph, never the fill. The toast gives a fill of 1.32:1 in light and 1.14:1 in dark with the border at 14.18:1 and 6.54:1; the amber warning, a fill of 1.13:1 and 1.83:1 with a border of 8.87:1 and 9.51:1; the close chip, a fill of 1.15 in light with the glyph at 7.53:1 in both themes, and the literals are the ones styles.css:5374-5413 publishes. The boxes (.sec, .tree, .panel) stay on the kit's --border: they are containers, not controls.
A finding that holds for the whole kit: --border-strong and --border-stronger do not work as a control outline in the Moodle DS palette — it is the same colour, and it fails in light. A control outline is --text-secondary. A second one, from the same family: --text-success/--border-success and --text-warning/--border-warning are identical pairs in this palette too, so the distinction between them — which bites in palettes where they differ — does not exist here.
