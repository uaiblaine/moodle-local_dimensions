Known limit: any ancestor whose overflow is not visible clips the balloon — and overflow-x:auto forces the Y axis to auto as well, so horizontal scrolling clips top and bottom alike. That is why the evidence journey (a static list) may use a tooltip while the linked-course scroller may not: there, the state has to be written on the card itself. A balloon that needs to escape a scrolling container is a sign the information should be visible, not on hover.

Design System rules — tooltip

  1. Never the only carrier of the information. The tooltip accompanies text that is already in the DOM and was clipped visually. If the value exists only in the balloon it is invisible to screen readers, to touch and in print — in that case, show it.
  2. No ARIA and no tabindex for the balloon's sake. Because nothing is hidden from assistive technology the balloon is decoration: no role="tooltip", no aria-describedby, no Esc handler. Adding tabindex="0" to a <span> just to make it focusable creates tab stops through a long list — do not. When the target is already a control, :focus-within covers it for free.
  3. Never pair it with title. The native balloon would sit on top of ours, on its own delay and in the operating system's styling. One or the other — title stays valid on a lone informational icon, like the "no completion tracking" marker in the tracker timeline.
  4. Clip on the child, balloon on the wrapper. .dim-tip carries no overflow; .trunc carries no position:relative.
  5. Above by default. .dim-tip-bottom only where the top is demonstrably clipped — first row of a list, top of a modal. No auto-detection: choosing at authoring time is cheaper than shipping a positioner.
  6. Short text. The balloon qualifies a label; it does not host a paragraph, a link or markup — content:attr() renders plain text by definition. An explanation that needs a link or a list is a modal, like "About this scale".
